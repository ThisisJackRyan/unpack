Why Glaciers Move

A glacier looks like it is standing still, but it is always moving downhill, just very slowly.[1] Some glaciers creep only a few centimeters a day, while others race ahead several meters.

The ice moves for two main reasons. First, the ice itself bends and flows under its own great weight, a bit like very thick honey.2 Second, the whole glacier can slide over the rock beneath it on a thin layer of meltwater.

ASIDE_PULL_QUOTE Ice flows like honey.

Measuring the speed

Scientists measure a glacier's speed by placing stakes in the ice and checking where they are a year later. Today, satellites can track the movement of whole glaciers from space without anyone setting foot on the ice.

When a glacier moves faster than usual, it is often a sign that more meltwater is reaching its base. That is one reason scientists watch glaciers so closely as the climate warms.[citation needed]
