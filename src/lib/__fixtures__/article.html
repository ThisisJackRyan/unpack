How Octopuses Change Color

By Sam Rivera · March 2, 2026

An octopus can change the color of its skin in less than a second. Scientists have studied this skill for many years, and they are still learning new things about how it works.

The secret is in tiny sacs of color called chromatophores. Each sac is filled with red, yellow, brown, or black pigment. Muscles around each sac squeeze or relax, and that makes the color spot grow bigger or smaller.

Why do they do it?

Octopuses change color to hide from predators, to sneak up on prey, and even to send messages to other octopuses. A pale octopus might be scared, while a dark red one might be ready to defend itself.

Some octopuses can even change the texture of their skin.
They raise small bumps called papillae, so they look like rough rocks or bumpy coral.

  • Chromatophores hold the pigment.
  • Iridophores reflect light.
  • Leucophores scatter white light.

Here is the strangest part: most octopuses are thought to be colorblind. Researchers think their skin might sense light directly, which could help them match their surroundings without seeing color the way we do.
